Messaging security, explained simply

How "Hey, how are you?" gets to your friend

WhatsApp and iMessage both lock your message on your phone before it leaves. Pick an app, then step through the journey to see what each server does and exactly what it receives.

The short version

Think of each message as a locked letter inside an envelope. Your phone locks the letter before it leaves, so no server ever sees "Hey, how are you?" as written.

Padlock directory

WhatsApp key server · Apple IDS

Gives your phone your friend's open padlocks. It never touches the message.

Post office

WhatsApp chat servers · Apple APNs

Carries the locked letter. It can read the envelope (who sent it, who it's for, and when) but not the letter.

File locker

WhatsApp media servers · iCloud

Stores locked photos and files. The key travels inside the locked message, so the locker can't open them.

When someone else can read it

Messages are locked while they travel. There are two common cases where the words can still end up readable by someone other than your friend.

Green-bubble texts

When iMessage can't find an Apple device for your friend, it sends a regular SMS through your mobile carrier. A plain SMS isn't locked, so the carrier can read it.

Cloud backups

A backup of your chat history may not be locked the same way.

  • WhatsApp backups to Google Drive or iCloud aren't locked unless you turn on End-to-end encrypted backup (Settings > Chats > Chat backup).
  • On iPhone, if iCloud Backup is on, Apple keeps a copy of the key to your messages. Turning on Advanced Data Protection (Settings > your name > iCloud) closes that gap.
